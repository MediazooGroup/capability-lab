# A second brief for reuse

This second, fictional brief tests whether the recipe transfers beyond managers. Use the same build-learning-asset skill.

Audience: newly appointed volunteer mentors. Time: ten minutes. Outcome: ask what support a mentee wants, summarise it back and agree one next step. Approved source: the mentoring coordinator's teaching note below. Review owner: mentoring coordinator.

Source note (fictional, approved only for this test): “At the start of a mentoring conversation, ask what support would be useful. Summarise the answer in the mentee's words. Agree one manageable next step and when to check in. Mentors help people think; they do not make promises on the programme's behalf.” Voice: plain, supportive English.

Reference outline: one minute to read a fictional conversation, two minutes to draft an opening question, four minutes to practise in pairs with a role swap, two minutes to discuss, one minute to agree the next step. Include two questions: “What did your summary help you check?” and “Was the agreed step something the mentee could choose?” The facilitator checks an open question, an accurate summary and an agreed next step. The mentoring coordinator reviews before use.

Review result: the reference outline meets the ten-minute constraint and practices the source behaviours. It makes no organisational promise or invented efficacy claim. It uses the same recipe structure without copying the manager scenario. A new model run still needs its own review; this reference is not a guarantee of identical outputs.
