# The first useful one-to-one

Draft for a learning lead to review. Northstar Services and the scenario below are fictional teaching examples. This is an authored reference answer using the build-learning-asset recipe and the supplied sources; it is not evidence of measured learning impact.

## Outcome

The manager asks an open question, listens before proposing a solution and agrees a next action with an owner and follow-up date. Source: framework.md.

## Ten-minute activity

| Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | Read the fictional scenario. |
| 2 | Prepare an opening question. |
| 4 | Rehearse in pairs, swapping roles after two minutes. |
| 2 | Discuss what changed after listening. |
| 1 | Agree the next action and a follow-up. |

Timing follows the ten-minute audience constraint in audience.md. The rehearsal structure applies framework.md. Pair timings include the role swap; the facilitator should keep the changeover brief.

## Scenario

Sam, a newly promoted manager, meets Alex for a first one-to-one. Alex says, “There is a lot going on. I’m not sure where to start.” Sam wants to help, but does not need to have every answer. These people and their words are fictional. The concern about having all the answers comes from audience.md.

## Practice

Ask: “Which part of the work would be most useful to talk through?” Listen and reflect back what you heard. Agree one next action, an owner and a follow-up date. An observer listens for those three behaviours. Source: framework.md.

## Two discussion questions

1. What did you hear that changed your first assumption?
2. How will you both know the next action happened?

These questions apply the supplied framework and the two-question format in voice.md.

## Facilitator note

Listen for an open question, a reflection before advice and a clear next action. If a manager moves immediately to fixing the problem, pause and invite a second attempt. Offer plain, supportive feedback. Sources: framework.md and voice.md.

## Before use

The learning lead checks relevance, timing, accessibility and source fit. Provide a written-response option for anyone who cannot take part in the spoken pair rehearsal. This is a design adaptation to review, not a requirement quoted from the sources. The activity has not been evaluated for effectiveness.
